Warren County Engineer Kurt Weber attended Tuesday night's Wayne Township Trustees meeting to talk about the North Warren County Transportation Plan, the regional study that includes two possible I-75 to I-71 routes through or near Waynesville. That's according to a detailed account posted afterward by Jeff Allen, who attended with Valerie Allen. Waynesville News did not attend the meeting, and the points below are Weber's remarks as Allen summarized them. Township minutes, once posted, will be the official record.

Allen wrote that Weber said he came because his office had received so much response from Wayne Township. He told the audience the study had received 480 surveys so far.

## What Weber said, according to the account

- **It's early.** The study's first phase began in April and won't be complete until July 2027. The project's posted schedule calls for recommendations on which ideas to study further by winter 2026–27 ([our Nov. 1 story](/stories/2026-11-01-north-warren-transportation-plan/) has the schedule and maps).
- **Public input matters.** Phase 1 looks at improvements across many projects, including the possible connector. Public input will be one of the main factors in deciding which options, if any, carry into Phase 2.
- **The push comes from Lebanon.** The need for a connector comes mainly from Lebanon, where truck traffic on S.R. 63 and S.R. 123 has grown. Weber said Lebanon City Council asked his office to look at solutions, and he told them "we'll look at it but no guarantees."
- **Phase 2 isn't funded.** A second phase would start in July 2027 and produce a long-term strategy focused on safety and capacity, but only if funding comes through. That money is not secured yet.
- **Nothing is funded for construction.** "If we were to move forward at all, it is a multi year project that has many, many hurdles in front of it," he said, as Allen quoted him.
- **Option 2 would be hard to build.** Northern Option 2, the new road across Wayne Township and the Little Miami River, would need a bridge that would be very expensive and very challenging. Weber cited its complexity, its cost and state and federal scenic-river requirements. The Little Miami is a designated State and National Scenic River.
- **A truck study is underway.** A separate origin-destination study is tracking where trucks start and end their trips. Its results will also factor into decisions.
- **Others share the decision.** The Ohio-Kentucky-Indiana Regional Council of Governments (OKI) and the Miami Valley Regional Planning Commission would be part of the decision-making group, along with the county.
- **More public comment is coming.** Weber said his office will keep reporting on Phase 1 and later work, with many more chances for public comment.

Allen wrote that several people in the audience spoke and asked questions, and that the project team seemed surprised by how much public interest there has been.
