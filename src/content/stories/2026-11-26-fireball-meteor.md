A fireball lit up the sky around **7:40 p.m. Wednesday, Nov. 25**, and more than 1,300 people reported it to the American Meteor Society, which tracks sightings of unusually bright meteors.

**What witnesses saw.** The society's fireball viewer groups 1,323 reports into a single event at 00:40 Universal Time on Nov. 26, which is 7:40 p.m. Eastern on Wednesday. Witnesses described a flash lasting about three and a half seconds on average, and their brightness estimates averaged about as bright as a full Moon. The reports came from across Ohio, Indiana and Michigan, with more from Kentucky, West Virginia and Ontario, and the society's estimate places the meteor near the Detroit area. That's far enough away that people in southwest Ohio would have seen it low in the northern sky.

**Around Cincinnati,** WLWT reported Wednesday night that multiple people saw a bright streak across the sky. In Northeast Ohio, Cleveland 19's First Alert Weather Team called it a fireball meteor and noted that two meteor showers, the Northern Taurids and the Leonids, are active this time of year.

**If you saw it,** you can add your own sighting with the American Meteor Society's online report form. The society uses witness reports from different places to estimate a fireball's path.
