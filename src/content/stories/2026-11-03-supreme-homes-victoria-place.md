A Brookville-based builder has pitched a housing development on land off Old Stage Road that would connect to the Victoria Place subdivision, according to reports in the last two Waynesville Village Council packets. No application has been filed and Council has taken no action.

## What's been proposed

Village Manager Gary Copeland wrote in his report for Council's [Oct. 19 meeting](https://www.villageofwaynesville.org/media/site/meetings/Council%20Agenda%2010.19.2026.pdf) that he met Oct. 14 with Mike Watkins of Supreme Homes, who presented a Planned Unit Development plan "for a development on Old Stage that connects to the Victoria Place subdivision." According to the report, Watkins is proposing three home styles:

- about 13 larger homes valued at $800,000 and up;
- about 25 to 30 smaller homes valued at $350,000 to $400,000, aimed at senior buyers, in areas that "will require variances";
- "executive houses (condominiums)" on the opposite side of Somerset Lane — three or four buildings of five to 10 units each, valued at $250,000 to $300,000.

Copeland wrote that the plan would include green space, sidewalks "and other Village Council-recommended requests," and that Watkins asked whether the village would be interested in a Tax Increment Financing (TIF) partnership. TIF lets a local government direct the increase in property taxes from new development toward public improvements such as roads and utilities.

The draft concept plan in the packet (above), dated Sept. 1, 2026, shows somewhat different numbers: 40 homesites in three lot sizes — 13 "Masterpiece" lots (100 by 130 feet), 14 "Designer" lots (80 by 130 feet) and 13 "Designer 40" lots (50 by 130 feet) — along two new streets running east from Old Stage Road. The map does not show the condominium buildings described in the report.

## What Council asked

At the Oct. 19 meeting, Copeland told Council that much of the roughly 40 acres "cannot be developed due to the property's undulation, making it unbuildable," according to the draft minutes in the [Nov. 2 Council packet](https://www.villageofwaynesville.org/media/site/meetings/Council%20Agenda%2011.02.2026.pdf). He said Watkins would prepare official drawings if he felt Council was receptive, but wanted to know Council was supportive before taking on that expense.

Council member Nation asked about the TIF request; Copeland said he had directed Watkins to speak to Council directly about it. Council member Gallagher asked about a lot by Church Street. Copeland said both parcels are part of the deal and that Watkins sees townhome-style "executive homes" as the only way to develop that parcel, since "it would not be worth the expense of infrastructure for just a few homes."

Gallagher also asked whether there had been talk of donating land for a park. Copeland said the property owners "did not seem responsive" to donating property, and Nation noted the land is an estate and that all parties would have to agree.

## What's next

Watkins was scheduled to present the plan to Council's Public Works Committee at 6 p.m. Monday, Nov. 2, ahead of that night's Council meeting. Minutes of that meeting have not been posted yet.

The builder's website, cited in the manager's report, is [supremecustomhomes.net](https://www.supremecustomhomes.net); the report points to George Town Village in Eaton as an example of the company's work.
