A regional transportation study is weighing new ways to connect I-75 and I-71 across northern Warren County, and two of the six early options run through or just past Waynesville.

The North Warren County Transportation Plan is being developed by the Ohio-Kentucky-Indiana Regional Council of Governments (OKI) with the Warren County Engineer's Office, the Warren County Transportation Improvement District and the Miami Valley Regional Planning Commission, with Burgess & Niple as consultant. The study area covers the northern half of the county, including the villages of Waynesville and Harveysburg and Wayne and Massie townships.

The options were shown at a public open house Oct. 28 at the Warren County Career Center, and the display boards are posted on the [project website](https://northwarrencountytransportationplan-burgessniple.hub.arcgis.com/pages/2156fd5cba494a4788d296ac591ce0c4). The boards stress that the options "are intended to give the general location of where a new roadway could go and do not represent final alignments," and that, based on public feedback, the project team "will select a few options to evaluate in the next phase of the study."

## Two options in the Waynesville area

**Northern Connection, Option 1** uses existing roads only. On the project's map it runs from I-75 at Middletown east along Old S.R. 122 to US 42, northeast on US 42 into Waynesville, then east on S.R. 73 through Corwin, across Caesar Creek Lake and through Harveysburg, ending at the S.R. 73 interchange on I-71 (map above).

**Northern Connection, Option 2** follows the same S.R. 122 corridor to US 42, then leaves existing roads southwest of the village. A new roadway would run southeast across Wayne Township, crossing Corwin Road and the Little Miami River just south of the Caesar Creek Gorge area, then Oregonia, Murray and Jeffery roads, meeting I-71 near Jeffery Road.

The other four options are farther south: two "central" options near Lebanon, and two "southern" options using a future I-75/Millikin Road interchange near Mason.

## The S.R. 73/US 42 intersection

Separately, the study lists 10 "high priority locations" for improvements. Second on the list is the intersection of S.R. 73 and US 42 in Waynesville. The project boards explain: "Closely spaced intersections between Main Street and US 42 contribute to congestion and safety concerns. This congestion reduces intersection efficiency."

Across the region, the boards list the kinds of fixes the study is considering: intersection improvements, roundabouts, corridor widening, intersection realignment, new connections, interchange improvements, and pedestrian and bicycle paths.

The study's freight board notes the need to balance freight movement "with the needs of growing residential communities," adding that "future improvements could help direct freight traffic away from community centers."

## What happens next

According to the project schedule, data collection was in spring 2026, goals were set over the summer, and possible solutions are being identified and evaluated this fall. Recommendations to advance for further study in a second phase are due in winter 2026–27. The boards say having a plan in place can also help local governments compete for state, regional and federal funding.

## How to comment

The project's [online survey](https://northwarrencountytransportationplan-burgessniple.hub.arcgis.com/pages/f27bc61005b842439ceb569b82c0b9cf) is open through **Tuesday, Nov. 17**. It covers the I-75 to I-71 options and the priority intersections. A second public open house, focused on recommended alternatives, is planned later in the study.
