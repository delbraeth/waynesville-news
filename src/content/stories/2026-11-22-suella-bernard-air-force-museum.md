Four days after D-Day, a nurse from Waynesville flew into France.

On June 10, 1944, 2nd Lt. Suella Bernard "of Waynesville, Ohio" was one of five Army nurses who "became the first Allied women to land in France," Stars and Stripes reported two days later. With a Ninth Air Force evacuation unit, they flew C-47s from England to dirt airstrips in the Cherbourg peninsula beachhead and brought 14 stretcher cases back to Britain the same day: wheels up at 8:49 a.m., back by 1:10 p.m.

Nine months later she flew a mission few nurses ever did. On March 22, 1945, near the Rhine bridgehead at Remagen, Germany, a medical air evacuation squadron loaded 26 patients onto two CG-4A gliders, which low-flying C-47s snatched off the ground and towed to a holding unit at Dunstekoven. The squadron commander, a doctor, rode with the patients in one glider. Then-1st Lt. Bernard rode in the other. The trip took nine minutes, instead of about four hours by ambulance. The jolt on takeoff snapped a litter rope and left three of her patients dangling, but she and a soldier reattached it, according to an Air Force Medical Service history. She called the landing "fairly smooth," even though a wheel collapsed and the glider came to rest against a fence.

The National Museum of the U.S. Air Force in Dayton describes her as the only nurse known to have flown on a glider combat mission in World War II and says she received the Air Medal. This month the museum is featuring her among the Ohioans in its "Ohio Serves" gallery highlights for the America 250-Ohio commemoration, recognizing "1st Lt. Suella Bernard of Waynesville, who cared for wounded soldiers during a glider evacuation mission in Germany."

The museum is at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base near Dayton; check its website for hours and visitor information.
