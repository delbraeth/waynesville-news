Four days after D-Day, a nurse from Waynesville flew into France.

On June 10, 1944, 2nd Lt. Suella Bernard "of Waynesville, Ohio" was one of five Army nurses who "became the first Allied women to land in France," Stars and Stripes reported two days later. With a Ninth Air Force evacuation unit, they flew C-47s from England to dirt airstrips in the Cherbourg peninsula beachhead and brought 14 stretcher cases back to Britain the same day: wheels up at 8:49 a.m., back by 1:10 p.m.

*"Two of the first flight nurses to make evacuation flights into Normandy after D-Day, Lts. Suella Bernard (left) and Marijean Brown (center) are greeted by Lt. Foster, their head nurse. They are holding poppies they brought back from the Normandy beachhead." (U.S. Air Force photo)*

Nine months later she flew a mission no other nurse is known to have flown. On March 22, 1945, two CG-4A gliders landed in a clearing near the Rhine bridgehead at Remagen, Germany, to pick up about two dozen severely wounded American and German soldiers. Once the gliders were loaded, low-flying C-47s snatched them off the ground and towed them away. The squadron commander, a doctor, rode with the patients in one glider. In the other, 1st Lt. Suella V. Bernard, who had volunteered for the mission, cared for the wounded on the way. An Air Force Medical Service history says the flight to an air evacuation holding unit took nine minutes, instead of about four hours by ambulance. The jolt on takeoff snapped a litter rope and left three of her patients dangling, but she and a soldier reattached it. She called the landing "fairly smooth," even though a wheel collapsed and the glider came to rest against a fence.

Flight nurses like Bernard were volunteers. The planes that carried the wounded also hauled supplies, so they couldn't display the Red Cross, and nothing marked them as noncombat flights. About 500 Army nurses served in 31 medical air evacuation squadrons during the war. Of the 1,176,048 patients they evacuated by air, only 46 died en route, and 17 flight nurses lost their lives, according to a 2013 Air Force history from the museum.

According to the National Museum of the U.S. Air Force in Dayton, she "became the only nurse known to have participated in a glider combat mission during World War II," and received the Air Medal for it. The museum's November "Ohio Serves" gallery highlights, part of the America 250-Ohio commemoration, include "1st Lt. Suella Bernard of Waynesville, who cared for wounded soldiers during a glider evacuation mission in Germany," according to Vintage Aviation News.

The museum is at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base near Dayton; check its website for hours and visitor information.
