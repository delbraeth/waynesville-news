# Planning note: local feature story ideas (2026-11-22)

**Status:** Idea list from the editor and Claude, 2026-11-22 (topics 20–25 added the same day). Nothing here is
verified yet. Details marked "(verify)" are from memory: check every fact
against the organization's own page, an original source, or a source the editor
sends before writing (see CLAUDE.md, "Always").

**How to publish one:** write it as a story in `src/content/stories/` with
`feature: true`, so it goes to Facebook with the dark "Feature" header (DEPLOY.md §6).
Publishing a new story needs the editor's go-ahead. Label lighthearted pieces
as lighthearted. Never name private living people. The editor approved
naming people from historical records such as the 1882 Crosswick account,
whose subjects died long ago.

## Founding and namesakes

1. **Who was "Mad" Anthony Wayne?** The Revolutionary War general behind the
   village and township names: the nickname, his 1794 win at Fallen Timbers,
   and the 1795 Treaty of Greenville that opened the area to settlement.
   Sources: Ohio History Connection, National Park Service.
2. **How Waynesville began (1797).** The founding and who platted the town
   (an English settler, verify), why Main Street looks the way it does, and the
   historic district's National Register listing. Sources: Waynesville
   Historical Society, the National Register nomination.
3. **The names behind the names.** Corwin, Harveysburg, Oregonia, Crosswick,
   Caesar Creek (the origin story is debated, so handle it carefully), and Mary L.
   Cook, the library's namesake.

## Quaker roots and freedom

4. **Waynesville's Quakers.** The Miami Monthly Meeting, Friends Home (now a
   museum) and the settlers who shaped the village. Source: Museum at the
   Friends Home.
5. **The Underground Railroad locally.** Only documented sites and people,
   with legend clearly separated from the record.
6. **Harveysburg's school for Black children (1831).** Often called Ohio's
   first free school for Black children (verify the wording). Source: the
   Harveysburg Community Historical Society.

## Lost places

7. **New Burlington, the village under Caesar Creek Lake.** Cleared for the
   Army Corps of Engineers' dam in the 1970s, and the subject of John Baskin's
   1976 book (verify). Could be a two-part series.
8. **Caesar Creek Pioneer Village.** The log buildings that were saved and moved
   there, and who keeps it going.

## Deep history

9. **Fort Ancient.** The hilltop earthworks near Oregonia, part of the
   Hopewell Ceremonial Earthworks World Heritage Site (verify the 2023
   inscription). What "Fort Ancient" means, and what it doesn't.
10. **450-million-year-old fossils at the Caesar Creek spillway.** Why the
    spot is famous, and the permit rules for collecting. Sources: ODNR, Army Corps.
11. **The Little Miami, a scenic river.** Its early state and national
    Scenic River designations (verify the years), and the rail trail through
    Corwin that was once a railroad.

## Festivals and fun

12. **History of the Ohio Sauerkraut Festival.** From a small village event
    to one of Ohio's biggest festivals: the first year (1970?, verify),
    founding stories, the strangest kraut foods. Source: festival organizers.
    Best timing: October, the week before the festival.
13. **How Waynesville became an antiques town.** When the shops took over
    Main Street, and the longest-running ones.
14. **The "most haunted village in Ohio."** Where the reputation came from,
    the ghost walks, and which buildings have stories. Light in tone, for Halloween.
15. **The Ohio Renaissance Festival in Harveysburg.** How a medieval village
    was built in a Warren County field.
16. **Red Stewart Airfield and the air show.** The grass runway, the skydivers,
    and the family behind it (verify).
17. **Fox Hollow Rodeo.** How long it has run and who runs it.

20. **History of the Warren County Fair.** How far back it goes (verify the
    first year), how it came to the Lebanon fairgrounds, 4-H and the junior
    fair, and old traditions and oddities. Source: the Warren County
    Agricultural Society or fair board, and the historical society. Best timing:
    the week before the July fair.
21. **The gliderport.** The Caesar Creek Soaring Club's gliderport near
    Waynesville (verify the name, location and founding year): how gliders fly
    without engines, who flies them, and whether visitors can take a ride.
    Source: the club. Best timing: spring or summer.

## Getting outside (a possible "Out and about" series)

22. **Hiking Caesar Creek.** A trail guide: the long perimeter loop, the
    shorter family hikes, the Caesar Creek Gorge State Nature Preserve and the
    best spots for spring wildflowers or fall color (verify trail names and
    distances). Sources: ODNR, Army Corps, Ohio DNR Natural Areas.
    Best timing: April or October.
23. **Horse trails.** Caesar Creek's bridle trails and horseman's camp (verify
    the mileage and camp details), the riding community, and trail etiquette
    for hikers and riders sharing paths. Ties in with the horse parades and
    the rodeo. Sources: ODNR, local riding groups (organizations only).
25. **Spring Valley Lake and the wildlife area.** The lake and marsh at the
    Spring Valley Wildlife Area, just north of Waynesville near the
    Greene–Warren county line (verify the acreage and which agency manages it,
    likely ODNR Division of Wildlife). Birding (migrating waterfowl, herons),
    the boardwalk or marsh trail (verify), fishing, and its link to the Little
    Miami Scenic Trail. Sources: ODNR, local birding groups (organizations only).
    Best timing: spring or fall migration.
24. **Paddling and biking the Little Miami.** Canoe liveries and the scenic
    rail trail through Corwin, as a companion to #11.

## Cryptids and oddities

18. **Reclaiming our cryptid.** Scheduled for **Saturday, May 29, 2027**, the
    145th anniversary of the 1882 account. Drafted and on hold in PR #170

    Warren County Historical Society. Open question: whether to add the

## Recurring series ideas

- **Then and now:** an old Main Street photo next to today's view of the
  same spot, with permission from the historical society.
- **Ask the Brief, history edition:** readers send in "why is it called…?" questions.
- **Business spotlight:** the longest-running shops (see the note in the brief
  draft template). This is coverage, not sponsorship.
